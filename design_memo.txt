How did recursive insertion work?

In this assignment, I learned how recursive insertion works in a binary tree. The insert() method starts at the root and checks if the current employee is the manager I am looking for.
If it finds the manager, it checks whether the left or right side is available. If the space is open, a new EmployeeNode is created and added there. 
If the manager is not the current node, the method calls itself again to search through the left and right sides of the tree.
This continues until the correct manager is found.


What challenges did I face when finding the right spot for a new employee?

One challenge I had was building the binary tree in the correct order. 
At first, I was confused about who should be added first, who should be the manager, and which employee should go under each manager. 
I also had to understand when an employee should be placed on the left or right side of the tree. 
Recursion was confusing at first because the method calls itself while searching for the correct manager. 
Testing the program step by step made the process easier to understand.
I started with the team lead, added employees under that person, and then added more employees under other managers. 
Printing the tree after adding employees also made it easier for me to see the hierarchy and check if everyone was placed in the correct position.

When might trees be preferable to other data structures in real-world systems?

I think trees are useful when information has a hierarchy. A company directory is a good example because employees can report to other employees. 
I can also relate this to teams at work, where we may have managers, supervisors, and assistants at different levels. 
A manager can be responsible for supervisors, and those supervisors can be responsible for other employees.
This creates a structure similar to the tree we created in this assignment. Trees could also be useful for file systems, organization charts, and decision-making systems. 
Compared with a regular list, a tree makes it easier to see who reports to whom and understand the relationships between different levels.
